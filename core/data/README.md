# Gazetteer data

These lists are embedded into the `tagging` binary at compile time. Extra lists can be added at
runtime through the `gazetteers` and `wordlists` fields of the config.

## File format

- Lines starting with `#` are comments, blank lines are skipped.
- `|` separates names. Spelling variants of the same entity share a line.
- A leading `~` marks names that are also everyday words in English, French, German or
  Luxembourgish (`~Berg`, `~Méck`, `~POST`). They score 0.55 instead of 0.85.
- Matching is case-insensitive on token boundaries. Names written fully in capitals (`CFL`,
  `BCEE`) are acronyms and only match with the same case. A match whose first letter is
  lowercase in the text scores 0.45, which is below the default threshold.

### `lu_places.txt`

`build_places.py` runs two SPARQL queries against the Wikidata Query Service:

1. All current communes of Luxembourg (instances of
   [Q2919801](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q2919801) without a dissolution date): 100 items.
2. Every item located in Luxembourg ([Q32](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q32)) whose type is a
   subclass of human settlement ([Q486972](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q486972)). This covers
   towns, villages, hamlets, lieux-dits and quarters of Luxembourg City.

Each item contributes its Luxembourgish, French, German and English labels. The raw query results
from 2026-10-23 are committed in `raw/`, so `python build_places.py --offline` reproduces the file
without network access. Without `--offline` the script fetches fresh results first.

The script also applies three hand-maintained lists:

- `AMBIGUOUS`: place names that collide with common words (`Berg`, `Born`, `Hamm`, `Italien`,
  `Jäger`, `Gronn`...). These are written on their own `~` line.
- `DROP`: labels of historical entities that are not places people live in.
- `EXTRA`: spellings in common use that Wikidata lacks (`Iernz` for Ernzen).

Known gaps: street names, Luxembourg City quarters missing from Wikidata, and spellings without
diacritics (`Ettelbreck`) are not covered.

### `lu_first_names.txt`

Compiled by hand from common Luxembourgish short forms and nicknames (Jhemp, Pol, Mett, Méck,
Fränz, Gusty, Jang, Lis...) plus first names that are frequent in Luxembourg. It is not
exhaustive, and it lists first names only. Surnames are left to the contextual model in a later
phase.

### `lu_organizations.txt`

Compiled by hand: public bodies, banks, hospitals, transport operators, research institutes and
major employers, with their acronyms and their French, German and Luxembourgish names where they
are in common use.
