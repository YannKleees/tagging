# Gazetteer data

These lists are embedded into the `tagging` binary at compile time. Extra lists can be added at
runtime through the `gazetteers` and `wordlists` fields of the config.

## File format

- Lines starting with `#` are comments, blank lines are skipped.
- `|` separates names. Spelling variants of the same entity share a line.
- A leading `~` marks names that are also everyday words in English, French, German or
  Luxembourgish (`~Berg`, `~Méck`, `~POST`). They score 0.55 instead of 0.85. After an indefinite
  article or a quantity (`eng Méck`, `e Kilo Mett`) they score 0.45.
- A leading `?` marks names that need more evidence than the word itself (`?Rose`, `?Orange`,
  `?Essen`). Alone they score 0.3, below the default threshold. A first name counts with a surname
  or a title next to it, a place or organization after a cue word (`in Essen`, `bei Orange`, 0.65).
  In `family_names.txt`, `?` marks family names that are everyday words; they do not name a
  business (`Hotel Zimmer`).
- Matching is case-insensitive on token boundaries. Names written fully in capitals (`CFL`,
  `BCEE`) are acronyms and only match with the same case. A match whose first letter is
  lowercase in the text scores 0.45, which is below the default threshold, unless the whole line
  is written without capitals (0.6 for unambiguous names).

The generated lists depend on each other. To rebuild everything from the committed raw data:

```
python build_common_words.py     # needs huggingface_hub and pyarrow, downloads about 1 GB once
python build_places.py --offline
python build_world.py --offline
python build_names.py --offline
```

Without `--offline` the scripts fetch fresh Wikidata results into `raw/` first.

### `lu_places.txt`

`build_places.py` runs two SPARQL queries against the Wikidata Query Service:

1. All current communes of Luxembourg (instances of
   [Q2919801](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q2919801) without a dissolution date): 100 items.
2. Every item located in Luxembourg ([Q32](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q32)) whose type is a
   subclass of human settlement ([Q486972](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q486972)). This covers
   towns, villages, hamlets, lieux-dits and quarters of Luxembourg City.

Each item contributes its Luxembourgish, French, German and English labels. The raw query results
from 2026-10-23 are committed in `raw/`, so `python build_places.py --offline` reproduces the file
without network access. Without `--offline` the script fetches fresh results first.

The script also applies three hand-maintained lists:

- `AMBIGUOUS`: place names that collide with common words (`Berg`, `Born`, `Hamm`, `Italien`,
  `Jäger`, `Gronn`...). These are written on their own `~` line.
- `DROP`: labels of historical entities that are not places people live in.
- `EXTRA`: spellings in common use that Wikidata lacks (`Iernz` for Ernzen).

Known gaps: street names, Luxembourg City quarters missing from Wikidata, and spellings without
diacritics (`Ettelbreck`) are not covered.

### `lu_first_names.txt`

Compiled by hand from common Luxembourgish short forms and nicknames (Jhemp, Pol, Mett, Méck,
Fränz, Gusty, Jang, Lis...) plus first names that are frequent in Luxembourg. It is not
exhaustive, and it lists first names only. Surnames are left to the contextual model in a later
phase.

### `lu_organizations.txt`

Compiled by hand: public bodies, banks, hospitals, transport operators, research institutes and
major employers, with their acronyms and their French, German and Luxembourgish names where they
are in common use.

### `given_names.txt` and `family_names.txt`

`build_names.py` counts, for nine countries of citizenship (Luxembourg, Belgium, France, Germany,
Portugal, Italy, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Spain), how many people in Wikidata carry
each given name (P735) and each family name (P734). The counts are committed in
`raw/given_names.csv` and `raw/family_names.csv`.

- A given name is listed with 15 people overall or 2 with Luxembourgish citizenship. It gets a `?`
  if it is shorter than three letters, an everyday word, or also a listed place or organization
  (`Paris`, `Florence`).
- A family name is listed with 8 people overall or 1 with Luxembourgish citizenship. An everyday
  word needs 40 (`Koch` stays, `Kuchen` goes).
- A family name never matches on its own. It extends a first name (`Jhemp Kieffer`) or follows a
  title, and an unlisted capitalized word after an unambiguous first name is taken as the surname
  with a lower score (0.7).

Known gaps: the counts reflect who has a Wikidata entry, mostly public figures, and are skewed
towards men and towards older generations. First names of recent immigration are thin.

### `world_places.txt` and `world_organizations.txt`

`build_world.py` takes sovereign states, cities with an article in at least 90 Wikipedia language
editions, populated places within 110 km of Luxembourg City, and organizations of about 25 types
(companies, banks, universities, international bodies) with an article in at least 50 editions.
`EXTRA_ORGS` adds large employers in Luxembourg that fall below that threshold (Deloitte, KPMG,
PwC...).

### `common_words.txt`

`build_common_words.py` reads about 150 million characters of Wikipedia text per language. A word
is an everyday word if it is mostly written in lowercase, or, for German and Luxembourgish nouns,
if it often follows a determiner. Words that are frequent names as well (`rose`, `richter`) are
marked with `~`: the lists above treat them as ambiguous, and the NER post-processing only drops
them after a determiner.

Known gaps: Luxembourgish coverage is thin, and words that Wikipedia mostly uses as surnames
(`Baker`, `Carpenter`, `Shepherd`) are missing.
